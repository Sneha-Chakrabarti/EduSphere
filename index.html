Chapter 1: Electric Charges and Fields

1.1 Introduction

Electrostatics deals with the study of charges at rest. Electric charge is an intrinsic property of matter responsible for electric forces. Charge is quantised and conserved.

1.2 Coulomb's Law

The electrostatic force between two point charges q₁ and q₂ separated by distance r in vacuum is given by:

F = k · q₁q₂ / r² where k = 1/(4πε₀) = 9 × 10⁹ N·m²·C⁻²

This force acts along the line joining the two charges. It is repulsive for like charges and attractive for unlike charges.

1.3 Superposition Principle

The total force on a charge due to multiple other charges is the vector sum of the individual forces:

F_total = F₁₂ + F₁₃ + F₁₄ + ··· + F₁ₙ
  • Forces add as vectors, not scalars
  • Each pair force is independent of other charges present
  • Valid for any number of charges

1.4 Electric Field

The electric field E at a point is defined as the force per unit positive test charge placed at that point:

E = F / q₀ (SI unit: N·C⁻¹ or V·m⁻¹)

Electric field lines represent the direction of E. They originate on positive charges and terminate on negative charges.

1.5 Gauss's Law

The total electric flux through any closed surface is equal to the net charge enclosed divided by ε₀:

∮ E · dA = Q_enc / ε₀

Gauss's law is particularly useful for calculating E in situations with high symmetry: spherical, cylindrical, and planar charge distributions.
